# JOB HUNT: What is the purpose of the "Have you ever applied to this company before" job application question? How should I answer that question if I don't remember, or I might have but unsure?

Employers ask “Have you ever applied to this company before?” to see if you’re already in their system, review any past notes about you, and understand your level of interest and history with them. It is not a trick question, but they do expect an honest, reasonable answer.[^1]

## Why companies ask this

- To pull up past applications and interview feedback in their applicant tracking system so they don’t start from zero if you were considered before.[^1]
- To spot serious issues noted previously (for example, no‑showing or unprofessional behavior) that might affect whether they move forward now.[^1]
- To understand your long‑term interest in the company and whether your profile or their needs may have changed since a prior application.[^1]


## How to answer if you’re unsure

If you genuinely do not remember, it is acceptable to say so clearly and briefly. The key is to avoid an obvious lie (e.g., saying “no” when you’ve recently interviewed there) and to give them enough information to match you in their system if needed.

### For an online form with only Yes/No

Options, in order of safety:

- If you’re *pretty sure* you have never applied there (or only maybe sent a generic resume years ago):
    - You can select **No**, especially if any prior contact would be very old, minor, or through a third‑party job board.
- If you *suspect* you might have applied or interviewed there in the last few years, but don’t remember details:
    - Select **Yes** and, in any free‑text box nearby (like “Additional information”), write something like:
        - “I may have applied previously but do not recall the exact role or date; it was likely several years ago.”


### For applications with a comments box

Use the comments field to be transparent and specific enough to be credible:

- “I applied to a role here a few years ago, but I don’t recall the exact title or date. My experience has grown significantly since then.”
- “I may have submitted an application through a job board in the past; I don’t have the exact details, but I’m very interested in opportunities with your company now.”


## If you remember you applied, but not the details

- Say **Yes**, then add a simple note:
    - “Yes, I applied for a marketing coordinator role around 2021.”
    - “Yes, I previously interviewed for a software engineer position, approximately mid‑2023.”
- You do *not* need to explain why you were rejected unless they ask; if they do, frame it as growth:
    - “At that time I had less experience with X; since then I’ve done Y and Z, which makes me a stronger fit now.”


## Practical rule of thumb

- When in real doubt:
    - Long‑ago, vague, or one‑off resume drops → selecting **No** is usually low‑risk.
    - Recent or memorable applications or interviews → lean **Yes** and add a brief clarification.
- Consistency matters: if you said in a previous interaction “I interviewed here last year,” but now check “No,” that inconsistency could hurt you more than admitting a prior rejection.
